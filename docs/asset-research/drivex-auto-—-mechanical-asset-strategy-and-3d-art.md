# Drivex Auto — mechanical asset strategy and 3D artist brief

Status: Strategy B approved by the user on 13 October 2026: CarConcept exterior plus separate high-quality mechanical scenes. Asset choices and production work remain unapproved; nothing commissioned. Planning only in Strawberry; Google Antigravity is a separate development workstream. No purchases, new asset downloads, modelling, edits to existing project or website implementation in this stage.

## 1. Decision recommendation
Recommend Strategy B: keep CarConcept as a conditional exterior/hero/panel-motion candidate, and build a small family of standalone mechanical scenes. Use the same studio lighting, material language, composition and transitions so they read as one premium experience. Present them as illustrative service assemblies, not literal anatomy extracted from CarConcept. Keep all 14 service categories accessible in ordinary content; only animate when it improves understanding.

Do not pack every assembly into one model or load everything at once. Do not use texture quality to disguise inaccurate mechanics. If the eventual brief requires an anatomically consistent exploded full-car transformation, switch to Strategy A and commission a unified generic vehicle; that requirement cannot be met convincingly by simply combining arbitrary downloaded parts.

CarConcept remains subject to removing/replacing all restricted marks, CC BY attribution, quality-reviewed lighting and mobile optimization. Its original asset remains unchanged. Prior file inspection and rendering evidence: carconcept-inspection/REPORT.md and carconcept-viewer/RENDERING-REPORT.md in this workspace.

## 2. Strategy comparison
| Dimension | A: unified detailed vehicle | B: exterior plus mechanical scenes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual coherence | Best potential for continuous anatomically credible teardown | Strong potential if art-directed; transitions must clearly distinguish illustrative scenes |
| Availability | No inspected free complete asset meets all requirements | Exterior verified; several free component leads, major gaps remain |
| Modelling | High: vehicle fit, underside, systems, rig and all assemblies | Medium-high: hero cleanup plus bounded components, no fictitious vehicle fit |
| Mobile delivery | Large authoring source; requires modular exports/LOD, not one monolithic download | Natural per-scene lazy loading and quality tiers |
| Licensing | Fewer upstream licenses if original commissioned work | Multiple provenance/attribution records; need consistent commercial rights |
| Quality risk | Expensive changes if vehicle type/anatomy changes | Mismatched detailing, scale or materials if sources are accepted without adaptation |
| Suitable use | Full-car exploded anatomy is a core requirement | Premium servicing storytelling and clear inquiries are the goal |

B is preferred for staged delivery and lower integration risk, not because all required art is already free. A remains the long-term option if continuity/anatomical accuracy outweighs time and cost.

## 5. Artist commission brief — proposed scope, not an order
### Objective
Produce a premium contemporary automotive visual family for mobile-first service storytelling. Precision, silhouette, realistic material response and readable anatomy matter more than maximal polygon count. Charcoal/yellow direction is confirmed; exact Drivex logo/colors await supplied brand files. Do not recreate GoMechanic branding, layouts or copy. Do not invent real vehicle compatibility, workshop facts, prices or contact details.

### Art direction and anatomical contract
Use a unified studio treatment and plausible material roughness: painted steel, cast metal, machined surfaces, rubber, fabric, glass, plastic and fluids only where needed. No baked lighting highlights in base-color maps. Keep dirt/repair states controlled and illustrative. Choose one generic passenger-vehicle mechanical architecture for each coherent assembly, with review by an automotive specialist. If assemblies do not literally fit CarConcept, keep them in separate scenes and label them illustrative. Do not imply that a four-cylinder cutaway is physically inside a futuristic concept body.

### Mandatory deliverables
- Editable .blend authoring sources with linked resources resolved, or agreed equivalent plus reproducible Blender export route.
- High-detail source where useful for baking; optimized exported GLB per assembly, not one all-assets blob.
- Texture source/export maps and documented color-space/channel packing; no embedded proprietary fonts/labels or unaudited branded surfaces.
- Named scene nodes, part IDs, parent hierarchy and part-to-service mapping manifest.
- Correct metric scale, axis convention, reset/rest transforms and pivot/hinge manifest with local rotation axes, limits and direction.
- Rest/closed, open/exploded and reassembled states; named clips only for approved actions. Bake constraints to supported keyframes; verify GLB playback rather than relying on Blender-only drivers.
- Mobile quality tiers with meaningful silhouette/material retention; compression settings and decoded image comparison.
- Licensed poster images and optional bounded image-sequence fallback, generated from the same approved scenes.
- License/provenance/attribution manifest for every source model, texture, HDRI, font, photo and derivative. SHA-256/revisions/source URLs; applicable license texts.
- Technical README covering export, loaders/decoders, disposal, expected limitations and validation results.

### Specific pivot/rig rules
Doors: door-local hinge carrying glass/handle/mirror/interior. Hood: proper hinge, attached underside/headlight behavior intentional; no floating parts. Wheels: steering parent separate from rolling axis; disc rotates with hub, caliper stays fixed to knuckle. Suspension: linked coherent rest/compression poses, tie rod behavior and clearance. Engine: separate service points; moving internals only if requested, mechanically consistent phase relationship. Battery: independent terminals/clamps/retention and safe illustrative removal sequence. Clutch: shared shaft axis, separate flywheel/disc/pressure plate/release bearing and bounded exploded offsets. Windshield/headlights/body: independent replacement assemblies only when the scene requires them. Avoid animating destructive glass fracture unless explicitly scoped.

## 6. Mobile budgets and quality acceptance
These are proposed starting engineering budgets, not measured guarantees or reasons to sacrifice visual quality. Calibrate after a rendered vertical slice on target devices.
- Visible assembly starting target: 50k–100k triangles; exterior mobile tier target <=150k if silhouette and finish survive. Exceptions require measured justification.
- Aim <=60 ordinary draw calls per isolated mechanical scene and <=100 for exterior; count actual renderer passes separately. Transmission/double-sided/shadows can increase cost.
- Begin with <=4 MB compressed transfer per mechanical scene and <=6 MB exterior tier; load only the first needed scene, not the entire inventory. CarConcept currently exceeds the exterior target and needs evaluation.
- Prefer 1K maps, 2K only for hero close-up/atlas where necessary; use KTX2 with reviewed quality, geometry compression where supported. Never infer GPU memory from download bytes.
- Progressive poster first; lazy scene loading; DPR cap/device tiers; pause offscreen/background; handle context loss, reduced motion and failed downloads. No essential information exclusive to 3D.
- Field targets remain p75 LCP <=2.5s, INP <=200ms, CLS <=0.1 where data exists; not asset-only promises.

### Visual acceptance
Compare approved reference renders to Three.js under matched lighting/camera. No noticeable seams, flipped normals, broken tangents, jagged silhouette at intended framing, warped UVs, distracting specular shimmer or missing material features. High-detail sources are not acceptance evidence unless the exported runtime matches quality. Render close and medium views in 360/390/430 layouts, portrait/landscape and desktop. Inspect all animated parts at endpoints and sampled intermediate poses for intersections, detached surfaces and implausible motion. No assumption of pixel-identical renderers.

### Technical acceptance
Run glTF Validator; record errors/warnings with disposition. Verify textures, animation clips, node IDs, compressed decoding and variants in the chosen Three.js version. Record file sizes, transfer/loading methodology, draw calls, active GPU memory where measurable, frame-time distribution and long stalls. Verify real iOS Safari/Android Chrome when devices are available; desktop narrow containers do not count as phones. Record target devices/network and quality tier; do not promise universal 60 FPS. No unauthorized third-party marks or missing attribution. Asset license bundle is a gate, not documentation to fill in later.

## 7. Estimated effort and cost structure
Planning estimates only, not supplier quotes. Assume one experienced real-time artist, bounded illustrative scenes, reusable materials and limited revision rounds. Accuracy review and actual source quality can materially change estimates.
- Exterior cleanup, brand texture replacement and optimized tier: roughly 3–7 artist-days.
- Candidate intake, retopology/material unification/rig fixes: roughly 1–4 days per usable candidate; reject poor candidates rather than investing blindly.
- Original battery/brake/light module: roughly 2–5 days each depending on approved close-up detail.
- Original AC, suspension/steering or clutch scene: roughly 4–10 days per module.
- Engine scene: roughly 5–12 days depending on exterior-only service points versus convincing internal cutaway.
- Quality/exports/fallbacks/cross-scene polish: reserve 20–30% beyond initial authoring effort.

Order-of-magnitude option A is high/very-high effort (potentially 8–16+ artist-weeks for a unified detailed vehicle and systems); bounded option B is medium-high (potentially 4–8+ artist-weeks for exterior plus selected scenes). These are overlapping workstream estimates, not sums, timelines or guarantees; scope and artist availability matter. Obtain itemized fixed-scope quotes later. Monetary budget = agreed artist rate × estimated effort + legal review/licensed references if needed + engineering/testing; no rate or price invented.

## 8. Staged implementation and approvals
1. Strategy review: approve A/B, literal vehicle anatomy vs illustrative separate scenes, priority scenes, target device tier and art direction. No execution assumed.
2. Candidate intake approval: authorize specific downloads only; verify files/licenses/marks/hierarchy/export. Reject NC/ND or unclear rights; evaluate SA obligations deliberately, not silently.
3. One art vertical slice: exterior-quality still plus one engine or wheel/brake scene with an approved reference render, GLB and fallback poster. Artist commissioning requires separate approval.
4. Browser quality/performance gate: verify visual fidelity, pivot motion, compression, device tiers and loading before multiplying assets. Review scope changes if quality/budgets conflict.
5. Asset production batches: battery/AC; suspension/steering; clutch; optional lights/repair-state panels. Maintain manifest and credits.
6. Prototype gate: separately authorize mobile hero + one scroll story with accessible services/contact configuration; do not expand automatically to production site.
7. Full specification approval: only then produce modular website source in Strawberry; transfer repo-ready assets/docs to Antigravity as a separate workstream. No claim of direct Antigravity access or import verification.
